Documentation & Diagnostic Guide

14-Stage Technical SEO Diagnostic Ladder

SEO Workbench executes audits in strict order of search engine processing priority. Foundational blockers must be resolved first because search engines cannot interpret downstream signals accurately until prerequisites are clear:

  1. 01 DNS & TLS: Resolvability, valid SSL/TLS certificates, non-www & HTTP canonical redirection.
  2. 02 Robots & Crawlability: Robots.txt directives, disallow blocks on critical assets or money paths.
  3. 03 Canonicals: Self-referential correctness, cross-domain canonical rules, absence of canonical chains.
  4. 04 Indexability: Meta robots, X-Robots-Tag, 4xx client errors, 5xx server drops.
  5. 05 Schema & Structured Data: Valid JSON-LD syntax, required entity properties, rich result eligibility.
  6. 06 Status & Redirect Hygiene: Elimination of redirect chains, 302 temporary redirects on permalinks.
  7. 07 Content & Thin Pages: Duplicate content identification, thin bodies (<300 words), semantic structure.
  8. 08 Heading Architecture: Single unique H1 per page, descriptive title tags, meta description coverage.
  9. 09 Internal Linking: Discovery of orphan URLs, click depth distribution, PageRank distribution flow.
  10. 10 Real-User Speed (CrUX): 75th percentile Core Web Vitals (LCP, INP, CLS) from Chrome UX Report.

Crawl Settings & Coverage Limits

In serverless deployments, crawls operate with an intelligent time budget (up to 45s) to guarantee fast responses without gateway timeouts:

  • Capped Crawls: If URLs remain queued, orphan claims are deferred to prevent false positives. Raise the Max Pages limit in Crawl Settings or scope the start URL to a specific section.
  • Robots.txt & Subdomains: Keep "Respect robots.txt" enabled for production auditing. Toggle "Include subdomains" only if regional or multi-tenant subdomains are in scope.

Crawl settings

Set the cap above your site's real page count. A crawl that stops early cannot assess orphans or duplicates — it reports what it reached, not what exists, so those checks switch themselves off rather than guess.

14-Point Diagnostic Ladder

The diagnostic order is the priority ladder: prerequisite technical layers must be resolved before higher-level content or speed optimizations can take effect.

Pages

Every URL the crawl reached. Pick one for the single-page review: eligibility, canonical, intent, on-page, internal links, structured data, rendering — in that order, leading with the highest-impact change.

Run a crawl first.

Intent match — stage 3

The one stage nothing can infer from your page alone. Tell it the query a page targets and it reads the live SERP in your Chrome, then compares what Google rewards against what your page is.

Keyword research

This runs before everything else. Without a keyword-to-URL map, "intent mismatch", "cannibalisation" and "content gap" are assertions rather than findings.

What the free tier can't give you. No search volume — nothing free returns it. Breadth counts how many independent autocomplete probes surfaced a phrase, which is a popularity hint, not volume. There's no live SERP scrape either, so clustering by SERP similarity — the correct method — isn't available here. Cluster by tokens as a first pass, then sample real SERPs before committing a page to a cluster. With Search Console connected, the landing-page clustering in that section is the stronger proxy: it's Google's own choice of page.

Search Console

The difference between observed and inferred. Without this connected the tool infers indexation from a crawl; with it, you read what Google actually decided.

Two ways in. The quick one needs no setup: in Search Console open Performance, set your date range, click Export → Download CSV, unzip it, and drop the files below. That is Google's own data with no OAuth, no Cloud project, no keys. The OAuth route below adds live refresh and URL Inspection if you want them later.

Performance

Field data from CrUX is the signal. Lighthouse lab scores are a debugging tool — useful for finding the cause, not for judging whether there's a problem. Both are shown, kept apart on purpose.

Core Web Vitals sit at rung seven. Real user value, small ranking tiebreaker, and never the answer to "why doesn't this rank". Fix eligibility, indexation and intent first.

Build

The execution half. Everything here produces something you can ship — markup, redirect rules, link plans, briefs.

Ship

Pre-launch checks, crawl-to-crawl comparison, and exports. Migrations are the most common cause of catastrophic traffic loss in the field, so this is the section that earns its keep.

Compare against a snapshot

Export

The Azure DevOps export writes one Task per finding, with severity mapped to Priority, owner and phase as tags, and testable acceptance criteria in the description. Recommendations that can't be accepted don't ship.

Brand context

Everything the AI writes reads from here. Fill it in once and the drafts stop sounding like drafts — leave it empty and the model invents a voice, which is why most AI copy reads the way it does.

The plan

Your findings, sequenced. The order is the priority ladder — effort only breaks ties inside a stage, never across them, so this won't quietly reorder itself around what's easy.

Social posts

Copy and artwork for each platform, at the right dimensions. The background is generated; the type is composed in real fonts, because image models can't spell.

Campaigns

A campaign records what you changed, when, and what the numbers were beforehand. Without that baseline you can't tell later whether it worked — you can only argue about it.

System check

The tool runs on free, keyless services. They're generous but not guaranteed, so this tells you what's actually reachable from this machine right now — the only place the answer is true.

Visibility

Where your pages turn up for the queries you care about.

Read this before you trust the numbers. No free service returns Google positions. If you connect Search Console you get real Google data for queries you already appear on, and that's always the better source. Failing that, this checks DuckDuckGo — a different index. Treat it as "am I visible at all", never as a Google rank.

Security headers

Graded separately from the SEO ladder, because it is a separate discipline. Only two of these headers touch search directly — but a compromised site loses rankings in a way no amount of on-page work recovers.

Run a crawl first. This reads response headers the crawl already collected, so it needs no extra requests.

Monitoring

Re-crawl on a schedule and report only what changed. Alerts fire on regressions, never on known state — a monitor that reports problems you've already accepted gets muted, and a muted monitor is worse than none.

Setup & keys

Everything works without any of these. Each one only adds something specific, named below. Keys are checked against the real API before being accepted — a key that saves but doesn't work is worse than none, because you debug it a week later.

Plan a new site

There is nothing to audit before a site exists, so the diagnostic half of this tool does not apply yet. What replaces it is the set of decisions that are cheap now and expensive after launch — most audit work on established sites is undoing choices someone made in week one.

People

Who owns which finding, and how much is on each of them. Work with no owner does not ship — that's the most common reason an audit produces no change.

This is assignment, not login. The tool runs on your own machine with no TLS, so a password screen here would protect nothing — anyone with the machine has the data files regardless. Rather than imply a guarantee that isn't there, this tracks ownership and load, which is the part that actually affects whether work gets done.

Behaviour

What people actually did on the page. A rage-click cluster or a dead click explains a conversion problem that rankings and Core Web Vitals never will.

Competitors

Crawl a competitor and compare how the two sites are built. Their HTML is public, so this needs no subscription and no API.

What this can and cannot tell you. It compares architecture, schema, content depth, internal linking and trust signals — the parts you can copy or beat. It cannot tell you their traffic, backlinks or rankings; those need a clickstream panel and a web-scale link graph, which is what an Ahrefs or Semrush subscription actually buys.

Server logs

Your server's own record of what Googlebot actually did. A crawl proves a page is reachable; only the log proves Google bothered to fetch it.

Nothing leaves your machine. The file is parsed in this process and never stored. Combined (Apache/nginx), IIS W3C, and JSON lines from a CDN are all detected automatically.

Where to find it: cPanel → Raw Access Logs; WP Engine → Logs; Cloudflare → Logpush; nginx → /var/log/nginx/access.log. A week is plenty; a month is better. Crawl the site first — the comparison between what Google requested and what exists is where most of the value is.

AI visibility

Runs your prompts through every reachable model and measures whether you are named, whether your domain is cited, and how the share of voice divides between you and everyone else the model mentions.
